# How to Get Your OSHA 10 Card: Step-by-Step Guide

> How to get a legitimate OSHA 10 card: choose the right course, find an authorized trainer, finish the 10 hours, and receive your DOL wallet card. Timelines, costs, and red flags.

URL: https://oshawithzahid.com/guides/how-to-get-osha-10-card.html
Author: Zahid Presswala, OSHA Authorized Outreach Trainer
Updated: 2026-10-27

To get an OSHA 10 card: (1) choose Construction or General Industry, (2) enroll with an OSHA-authorized Outreach trainer or an OSHA-accepted online provider, (3) complete all 10 contact hours over at least two days and pass the course assessment, and (4) receive your official Department of Labor wallet card, which trainers must issue within 90 days.

## Key takeaways

- There is no way to get a real OSHA 10 card in one day. OSHA caps Outreach training at 7.5 hours per day.
- Only OSHA-authorized trainers and OSHA-accepted online providers can issue DOL cards.
- You'll usually get a printable completion certificate right away; the plastic card follows.
- Pick Construction or General Industry based on the work you do, not the one that's cheaper.
- Keep your card safe. Replacements are limited and go through the original trainer.

## Step 1: Choose the right industry

OSHA 10 comes in two main versions, and the card says which one you took:

- **Construction** if you build, renovate, demolish, paint, roof, or do electrical, plumbing, or HVAC work on construction projects.
- **General Industry** if you work in a warehouse, plant, distribution center, healthcare facility, retail, or most other fixed workplaces.

A Construction card won't satisfy a General Industry requirement and vice versa. If you're not sure, our [Construction vs General Industry guide](https://oshawithzahid.com/guides/construction-vs-general-industry.html) breaks it down.

## Step 2: Find a legitimate trainer or provider

A real OSHA 10 card is a U.S. Department of Labor card issued through the **OSHA Outreach Training Program**. You can earn one in two ways:

- **In person** with an OSHA-authorized Outreach trainer. You can look up any trainer in [OSHA's trainer directory](https://www.osha.gov/dte/outreach/outreach_trainers). (Here's [Zahid's listing](https://www.osha.gov/dte/outreach/outreach_trainers?page=0&last_name=Presswala).)
- **Online** with one of a small number of OSHA-accepted online Outreach providers listed on OSHA's website.

**Red flags:** "Get your OSHA card today," prices that seem too good to be true, no identity checks, or a provider that can't name the authorized trainer behind the course. Fake cards are a real problem, and an employer or inspector can reject them.

## Step 3: Complete all 10 hours

OSHA sets rules that every legitimate course follows:

- **10 contact hours** of instruction, covering required topics plus electives.
- **No more than 7.5 hours per day**, so the course spans at least two days.
- **Online courses** must be completed within six months of starting, and include knowledge checks and a final exam.
- **In-person classes** require you to attend the whole course. Missing part of it means you don't get a card until you make it up.

In an on-site class, we usually schedule two consecutive days at your job site or shop. Your crew learns together, asks questions about their actual work, and nobody has to fight with a slow website.

## Step 4: Receive your DOL wallet card

When you finish, you'll typically get a completion certificate right away. The official plastic DOL card comes from the trainer or provider, and OSHA requires it to be issued **within 90 days**. Modern cards include security features that make them harder to fake.

When it arrives, check that your name is spelled correctly and the industry (Construction or General Industry) and course (10-hour) are right.

## After you get your card

An OSHA 10 card is a starting point, not the finish line. It shows you've had basic hazard awareness training. Your employer is still responsible for training you on the specific hazards and equipment you work with. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can I get my OSHA 10 card in one day?

No. OSHA limits Outreach training to a maximum of 7.5 hours per day, so a legitimate OSHA 10 course always spans at least two days. Anyone offering a same-day OSHA 10 card is not following OSHA rules.

### How long does it take to receive the physical card?

OSHA requires authorized trainers to provide cards to students within 90 days of course completion. Many students receive theirs sooner, and most providers give a completion certificate you can show an employer in the meantime.

### Is there a test for OSHA 10?

Yes. Outreach courses include knowledge checks and a final assessment. Online courses require a passing score on the final exam, and trainers verify that students participated in the full course.

### What if I lose my OSHA 10 card?

Contact the trainer or online provider who issued it. Replacement cards are limited and are generally only available for a set number of years after training, so contact them as soon as you notice it's missing.

### Can I get an OSHA 10 card in Spanish or another language?

Yes. Outreach courses can be taught in other languages by trainers who are fluent in them. Zahid teaches in English, Gujarati, and Urdu.
