How Much Does OSHA 10 and OSHA 30 Training Cost?

Prices vary a lot depending on format and group size. Here's what to expect and how to avoid paying for a card that isn't real.

ZP
By Zahid PresswalaOSHA Authorized Outreach Trainer · Updated October 27, 2026 · 3 min read
Quick answer

There is no official OSHA price. Cost depends on the course (OSHA 30 costs more than OSHA 10), the format (self-paced online, in-person, or on-site), and group size. On-site group training spreads the trainer's cost across the whole crew, so the price per worker drops as headcount grows. Prices that seem far too low, or promises of a card in one day, are warning signs of a fake card. For an on-site quote, contact Zahid Presswala through the booking form.

Key takeaways

  • There is no official OSHA price; each authorized trainer and provider sets its own.
  • In-person and on-site classes are priced per person or per group, depending on the trainer.
  • On-site group training usually gives employers the lowest cost per worker for crews of five or more.
  • Every legitimate course ends with an official DOL card, so compare total cost, not just the sticker price.
  • A deal that skips the 10 or 30 hours is not a real OSHA card.

How OSHA 10 and OSHA 30 pricing works

There's no official price. OSHA authorizes trainers and online providers, and each sets its own rates. Here's how the formats compare:

FormatOSHA 10OSHA 30Notes
Online (OSHA-authorized provider)Priced per personPriced per person, higher than OSHA 10Self-paced; limited to 7.5 hours per day
In-person public classVaries, usually more than onlineVaries, usually more than onlineLive trainer, fixed dates and location
On-site group classQuoted per groupQuoted per groupCost per worker drops as headcount rises

Prices change, and many providers offer discounts or volume pricing. For on-site training, request a quote with your headcount and location.

What affects the price

  • Course length. OSHA 30 is three times as long as OSHA 10 and costs more.
  • Format. Online is cheapest per person; live training costs more but includes a real trainer.
  • Group size. On-site classes spread the trainer's cost across everyone in the room.
  • Location and travel. Training far from the trainer's base may include travel.
  • Language. Finding an authorized trainer who teaches in your crew's language can be harder, but it pays off in understanding.

The real cost for employers

For a company, the sticker price is only part of the cost. Consider:

  • Paid time and lost production while workers train, which is the same whichever format you choose.
  • Travel time if you send people to an off-site class.
  • Tracking who finished an online course, and who didn't.
  • Effectiveness. Training that sticks prevents injuries and OSHA penalties, which can run into the tens of thousands of dollars per violation.

That's why many contractors and facilities choose on-site group training: one class, everyone done, no travel.

Don't pay for a fake card

If an offer sounds too cheap or too fast, it probably is. Warning signs:

  • An OSHA 10 finished in a single day, or OSHA 30 in under four days
  • No way to verify the trainer on OSHA's website
  • A "certificate" or plastic card that isn't the official DOL Outreach card

Read how to verify an OSHA card before you pay. You can verify Zahid on OSHA.gov.

Frequently asked questions

Is OSHA 10 free anywhere?

Sometimes. Some unions, apprenticeship programs, workforce agencies, and employers pay for OSHA 10 for their members or workers. OSHA itself doesn't sell the course; authorized trainers and online providers do.

Who pays for OSHA training, the worker or the employer?

It depends. When OSHA 10 or 30 is a condition of a job or project, employers usually pay. Workers sometimes pay on their own to qualify for jobs. Separately, when OSHA standards require hazard-specific training, the employer must provide it at no cost to workers.

Why does in-person training cost more than online?

You're paying for a live, authorized trainer's time, travel, and the ability to tailor the course to your work. For groups, that cost is shared, so the price per worker often ends up competitive with online courses.
