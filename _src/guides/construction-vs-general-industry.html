Two sets of OSHA rules

OSHA standards are split by industry. The two you'll hear about most:

  • 29 CFR 1926 (Construction): rules for construction, alteration, repair, painting, decorating, and demolition work.
  • 29 CFR 1910 (General Industry): rules for most other workplaces, including manufacturing, warehousing, healthcare, retail, and services.

Outreach courses are built around these standards, which is why OSHA 10 and 30 come in separate Construction and General Industry versions.

What each course focuses on

Construction

  • The Focus Four: falls, struck-by, caught-in or between, electrocution
  • Scaffolds, ladders, and stairways
  • Excavation and trenching
  • Cranes, rigging, and heavy equipment (more in OSHA 30)
  • Health hazards like silica, noise, and lead

General Industry

  • Walking-working surfaces and fall protection
  • Emergency action plans, fire prevention, and exit routes
  • Electrical safety
  • Hazard communication and chemical safety
  • Machine guarding, lockout/tagout, and powered industrial trucks (forklifts)

The maintenance gray area

This trips up a lot of facility teams. OSHA generally looks at the nature of the work:

  • Maintenance: keeping a building or equipment in its existing condition, such as replacing a motor with the same model or patching a roof. This is generally General Industry.
  • Construction: building, altering, or significantly upgrading, such as adding a mezzanine, re-wiring a production line, or remodeling an office. This is generally Construction.

If your maintenance staff regularly do both, it often makes sense to train them in General Industry and give the leads Construction OSHA 30, or vice versa. I'm happy to help you think it through when you request training.

Before you book: ask these three questions

  1. What does the requirement say? If a contract, site policy, or state law requires a card, it will usually specify the industry.
  2. Where does most of your work happen? A construction project or a fixed facility?
  3. What's your role? Workers take OSHA 10; supervisors take OSHA 30. See OSHA 10 vs OSHA 30.
