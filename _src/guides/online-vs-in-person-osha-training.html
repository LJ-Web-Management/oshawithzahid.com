When online training makes sense

  • You're one person who needs a card and has a reliable computer and internet connection.
  • Your schedule makes fixed class dates impossible.
  • You're comfortable reading English or Spanish technical content on your own.

Just make sure the provider is on OSHA's list of accepted online Outreach providers. Plenty of sites sell "OSHA training" that doesn't lead to a DOL card.

When in-person training is the better choice

You're training a crew

With online courses, every worker progresses at a different pace. Some finish in a week, some take months, and some never finish. With an on-site class, the whole crew is done in two days (OSHA 10) or four (OSHA 30), and you know exactly when everyone will have their completion record.

You want training that fits your work

An online module can't look at your scaffold, your trench, or your loading dock. A live trainer can tie every topic back to the equipment and hazards your crew sees every day, and answer the "what about when we..." questions that always come up.

Your crew speaks more than one language

People learn safety best in the language they think in. Being able to explain a lockout procedure or a fall arrest system in Gujarati or Urdu, and not just English, makes a real difference for many crews I train.

Retention matters to you

Clicking "next" through slides isn't the same as discussing a near-miss with your coworkers. In my experience, crews that train together also hold each other accountable afterward.

A hybrid approach

Some employers do both: online OSHA 10 for a new hire who starts between classes, and on-site OSHA 10 or 30 for the main crew and supervisors. Either way, follow up with toolbox talks and task-specific training.
