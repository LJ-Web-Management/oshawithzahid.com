Who enforces OSHA in Illinois?

Illinois runs a public-sector-only state plan. That creates two systems:

Employer typeCovered by
Private companies: contractors, manufacturers, warehouses, retailers, healthcareFederal OSHA
State agencies, counties, cities, villages, townships, school districts, public universitiesIllinois OSHA (Illinois Department of Labor)

Illinois OSHA's standards must be at least as effective as federal OSHA's, so the safety rules you'll learn in an OSHA 10 or 30 course apply on both sides.

Are OSHA 10 or 30 cards required in Illinois?

Unlike some states, Illinois doesn't have a broad law requiring every construction worker to hold an OSHA card. But that doesn't mean you can skip it. In the Chicago area, requirements usually come from:

  • General contractors who require OSHA 10 for everyone on site and OSHA 30 for supervisors.
  • Project owners such as hospitals, universities, airports, data centers, and large commercial developers.
  • Public agencies and municipalities that write OSHA training into bid documents and contracts.
  • Unions and apprenticeship programs that include Outreach training in their curriculum.
  • Insurers and safety programs that reward documented training.

If you bid on larger projects in Cook, DuPage, Will, Kane, or Lake counties, plan on needing cards for your crew.

Tip for contractors: Don't wait until a project starts to discover your crew needs cards. A legitimate OSHA 10 takes at least two days and OSHA 30 at least four, so build training into your schedule before mobilization.

OSHA's employer training duties still apply

Even when no card is required, OSHA standards require employers to train workers on specific hazards they face, such as fall protection, hazard communication, forklifts, respirators, and lockout/tagout. An Outreach course is a strong foundation for that, but it doesn't replace site- and equipment-specific training.
