What is OSHA 10-Hour Construction?

OSHA 10-Hour Construction is the most requested safety course on American job sites. It's part of OSHA's voluntary Outreach Training Program, and it gives new and entry-level workers a working knowledge of the hazards they'll face on a construction site, how to protect themselves, and what their rights are under federal law.

It is not a license or certification to perform a specific task. It is the card most general contractors, project owners, and unions in the Chicago area ask to see before a worker steps on site.

What the course covers

OSHA sets the outline for every Outreach course. The 10-hour Construction class includes:

TopicTimeWhat you learn
Introduction to OSHA2 hrsWorker rights, employer responsibilities, how to file a complaint, how inspections work
Focus Four hazards4 hrsFalls, electrocution, struck-by, and caught-in or between, the leading causes of construction deaths
Personal protective and lifesaving equipmentRequiredChoosing, wearing, and inspecting PPE
Health hazards in constructionRequiredNoise, silica, lead, chemicals, heat, and other exposures
Electives2+ hrsChosen for your crew, such as scaffolds, ladders and stairways, excavations, materials handling, or tools

Because Zahid chooses electives based on your work, a roofing crew spends its elective time on very different material than an electrical or concrete crew. See what's covered in OSHA 10 for the full breakdown.

Who should take OSHA 10 Construction?

  • New hires and apprentices starting their first construction job.
  • Laborers and tradespeople: carpenters, framers, drywall, concrete, roofing, masonry, electrical, plumbing, HVAC, painting, and demolition crews.
  • Workers on projects that require a card: hospitals, universities, data centers, public works, and most large commercial jobs in Cook, DuPage, Will, Kane, and Lake counties.
  • Remodelers and small contractors who want documented training for insurance and bids.

If you supervise others or are responsible for site safety, OSHA 30 Construction is the better fit. Not sure? Read OSHA 10 vs OSHA 30.
